Sashiko is a technique for reinforcing textiles, whether you're adding a patch or not, which boils down to a simple up-down-up-down line of stitches. That simplicity lends itself to economical geometric shapes, and from those shapes, beautiful repeating patterns can emerge.

I love sewing traditional Japanese motifs, but there are plenty of modern adaptations that fit the definition. Cats, boats, and rope patterns are all fairly easy to create. This technique is so universal that once you start noticing it, you'll find it cropping up in textile traditions all over the world.

I draw on a variety of sources to build my mending patterns. I hope you'll find some inspiration among my favourites, which I've gathered on my Pinterest board. See which one speaks to you, and embrace the beauty of even the simplest shapes.
